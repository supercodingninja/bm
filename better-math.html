How big is it?

Metric is used almost everywhere. Inches and feet are used in the USA.

Tiny words: your thumb is an inch. A giant step is a meter. A bag of sugar is a kilogram.

Metric vs. SAE tools

Wrenches come in two families. They look almost the same — but almost is not exact.

Tiny words: millimeter numbers go together. Fractions like 1/2 go together.

Careful: the wrong system can round off a bolt. When in doubt, use the exact size.

Spot the difference before grabbing a tool:

Metric bolts: numbers like 8.8 or 10.9 SAE bolts: fractions like 1/2 or 5/8
Metric = most modern cars, European & Asian vehicles, electronics SAE = classic American cars, older machines, lumber

Tip: digital calipers switch between metric and SAE instantly.

For grown-ups: the science behind this page

Color. Research on children's cognition found that red hampered performance on cognitive tasks by roughly 6% relative to grey, while no other color hurt — and blue environments support relaxed, creative thinking (Andrew Elliot's line of research). Green is the color of restoration: attention-restoration studies show even brief contact with green/nature refocuses a tired mind. That is why every palette here — light or dark, garden or farm — keeps warm low-glare surfaces, blue/green anchors, amber warmth, and reserves red-family tones for gentle "try again" moments only.

Rewards. Lepper, Greene & Nisbett (1973) showed that expected tangible rewards halved preschoolers' voluntary drawing afterward — the famous overjustification effect. A 1999 meta-analysis of 128 experiments (Deci, Koestner & Ryan) confirmed: expected tangible rewards reduce intrinsic motivation, while verbal encouragement increases it. So there are no points, stars-to-keep, streaks, timers, or prizes here — only living things that grow and words like "you figured it out."

Mistakes. Wrong answers never cost anything and can always be retried. A brain that is not afraid of being wrong is a brain that keeps trying — that is the entire goal.

Themes. Themes change the world, never the math. Neutral tends a garden; The Alpha Male works a farm — same calm colors in earth and crop tones, same gentle rules. Letting a child choose his world is autonomy, and autonomy feeds motivation.

Sync between devices. Progress is saved privately on each device. To move it: tap "send my garden" for a short code, or share the progress file — AirDrop from iPhone to iPad is the smoothest path. For the best experience on Apple devices, host this file (a free Netlify site works) and use "Add to Home Screen" on each device; then it opens fullscreen like a real app, and Control Center → Screen Mirroring puts it on the TV.

After a challenge round, encourage a one-minute "green break" — look out a window. It genuinely refocuses young attention.
